Glossary of loan terms

Loan documents are full of shorthand. This is what the words we use actually mean, in plain language.

Borrowing terms, in plain words

Loan documents are full of shorthand. This list explains the terms we use on this site, so you can read an offer with confidence rather than guesswork.

EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment)
The fixed amount you pay each month, covering both principal and interest, until the loan is repaid.
Principal
The amount you borrowed, before any interest or charges are added.
Interest rate
The cost of borrowing, expressed as a percentage per year. A reducing-balance rate is charged on the outstanding balance, so interest falls as you repay.
Flat rate
A rate calculated on the full original amount for the whole tenure, rather than on the reducing balance. A flat rate and a reducing-balance rate of the same number are not equivalent; the effective cost of a flat rate is higher.
APR (Annual Percentage Rate)
A single figure that expresses the yearly cost of a loan including interest and certain charges, so products can be compared like for like.
Processing fee
A one-time charge for assessing and setting up your loan. It is disclosed before you accept.
Prepayment
Repaying more than the scheduled instalment, or settling the loan early. Whether a charge applies depends on the product.
Tenure
The length of time over which you agree to repay the loan.
Disbursal
The point at which the loan amount is released to your nominated bank account.
Arrears
An amount that is due and unpaid after its due date.
Default
A serious and continuing failure to repay as agreed. Default can affect your credit record and lead to recovery action.
Credit bureau
An agency that records your borrowing and repayment history, which lenders may consult when assessing an application.
KYC (Know Your Customer)
The identity and address checks a lender must complete before providing a loan.
Cooling-off period
A short window after taking a loan in which you may cancel it and repay the principal without a prepayment penalty, where the product offers this.
Nodal officer
The named person responsible for handling escalated complaints and grievances.
