Ways to repay

Choose a repayment method that suits you, keep the due date in view, and know exactly what to do if a payment is going to be late. No surprises, no hidden charges.

Choose how you pay

Every method credits the same instalment. Pick the one that is easiest for you to keep on time.

Setting up auto-debit

Auto-debit is the simplest way to stay on time. You authorise it once, and the instalment is collected on each due date. Keep enough balance in the account a day before the date so the collection is not returned. You can change or cancel the mandate from your account at any time.

Changing a due date

If your income arrives on a different day, you can ask to move your due date so it follows your salary or harvest. Ask us before the change is needed, not after a payment is missed — it is a routine request, not a favour, and there is no charge for making it.

Paying early or part-paying

You can repay more than the scheduled instalment, or settle the loan early. Whether a charge applies depends on the product and is set out in your agreement. Interest is charged only on the outstanding balance, so paying earlier reduces what you owe. See Rates & Fees for the current schedule.

If a payment is going to be late

Tell us early. A short delay is far easier to resolve before the due date than after it. If you are in genuine difficulty, we can look at a revised repayment plan — see Recovery & Collections for how we handle a missed payment, the hours we call and the support available.

What a late payment can cost

A late instalment may attract a penalty calculated on the overdue amount only, and a failed mandate may attract a small charge. Both are listed on the Rates & Fees page and disclosed before you accept a loan.
