Dispute, Cancellation & Refund Policy

Last Updated: 24 November 2026

This policy explains how a disagreement, a cancellation and a refund are handled on the Gahonsh Freelancing marketplace at https://gahonsh-blip.github.io/. It applies to every marketplace engagement and to agency work quoted by Gahonsh. It forms part of the Terms & Conditions, so please read both.

1. Start with the record you already have

Almost every disagreement is a gap between what the brief asked for and what was delivered. Before you escalate, use the record on the site:

  • Read the project brief and the proposal you accepted. The agreed scope, bid amount and delivery window are on the project page and in your portal.
  • Check the milestone checklist on the contract. Each milestone has a defined deliverable, so you can see which one is late or short.
  • Use the contract message thread. A written request for a fix inside the agreed scope is faster than a dispute, and the freelancer is expected to answer it.

Every paid engagement includes the revision allowance stated in the agreement, plus 7 days of free bug support after delivery for defects in the delivered work. A bug is a fault in what was promised; a new requirement is a change of scope and is priced separately.

2. Cancelling before work starts

A client can withdraw a project from the feed at any time before a proposal is accepted. Nothing is owed and no contract exists. A freelancer can withdraw a proposal at any time before it is accepted.

Once a proposal is accepted the engagement becomes a contract, and either side can request cancellation from the contract card in the portal. Cancellation is available while the contract is active. The portal records who cancelled and why, and closes the contract so neither side is left waiting on a project that is not going ahead.

3. Cancelling after work has started

If work has begun, the milestone checklist decides what is owed:

  • Milestones already delivered and accepted stay payable. Work that was reviewed and signed off is not refundable because the engagement later ended.
  • Milestones not started are not payable. A client who cancels pays only for work already delivered.
  • A milestone in progress is settled between the parties on the proportion delivered. If you cannot agree, use the dispute process in section 5.
  • A deposit reserves delivery time and becomes non-refundable once work starts. Cancel before work starts and it is returned in full.

4. What is refundable, and what is not

Because payments move directly between the client and the freelancer, a refund is a payment the freelancer sends back, not a platform reversal. Gahonsh records the agreement and reviews the case; it does not hold or move your money. See section 6.

Refundable

  • A deposit or upfront payment for work that never started.
  • A milestone that was paid but never delivered, where the freelancer agrees or the review finds no delivery.
  • A payment made twice by mistake, or made to the wrong account on our instruction.
  • Agency work that Gahonsh accepted, quoted and then could not deliver.

Not refundable

  • Work delivered and accepted, including work accepted by silence after a review window you agreed to.
  • A change of mind after delivery, or a change of requirement that was not in the agreed scope.
  • Third-party costs already spent on your instruction, such as licences, fonts, stock assets or paid APIs.
  • Rush or priority fees once the priority slot has been used.

5. Raising a dispute

A dispute is a written request for Gahonsh to review an engagement. Either party can raise one from the contract card in the portal, or by writing to gahonsh@gmail.com with the project link. Include:

  • The project link or contract reference, and the milestone in question.
  • The agreed scope: what the brief asked for and what you expected.
  • What was delivered, with dates. Screenshots, files and the message thread are the useful evidence.
  • The outcome you are asking for: a fix, a partial refund, a full refund or cancellation.

We acknowledge a dispute within 2 business days and give a written view within 10 business days of receiving the evidence. Both parties are asked for their side before a view is given, and a request for information pauses the clock.

6. How we decide, and what we can actually do

We review the brief, the accepted proposal, the milestone checklist, the delivery record and the message thread. We look at whether the deliverable matches the agreed scope, whether it was delivered on the agreed date, and whether each side responded within a reasonable time. Our view is a written recommendation, not a court order.

Gahonsh does not hold client funds and does not operate an escrow account, so we cannot reverse a bank transfer, a card payment or a wallet transfer between two people. What we can do:

  • Set the contract to disputed in the portal, so the record shows the engagement is under review and neither side is asked to continue as normal.
  • Recommend a specific outcome: a fix, a partial refund, a full refund, or cancellation with nothing further owed.
  • Restrict or suspend an account that ignores the outcome, repeatedly breaches these terms, or behaves abusively.
  • Publish nothing about the case. Reviews and outcomes stay between the parties unless a party asks us to record a public outcome.

If a party is not satisfied with our view, they may pursue their own legal remedy. Nothing in this policy limits a right you have under applicable law.

7. Chargebacks and payment reversals

A chargeback filed with a bank or card issuer instead of using this process is a payment reversal we cannot see in advance. When one is raised, the account is restricted while the case is reviewed, and we provide the bank with the brief, the accepted proposal, the delivery record and the message thread. Filing a chargeback for work that was delivered and accepted may end the account.

8. Repeated disputes and abuse

Disputes are free and there is no limit on raising a genuine one. A pattern of disputes over work that was delivered, fake evidence, or a refusal to use the process at all is treated as a breach of the Terms & Conditions and can lead to a warning, a restriction or a suspension. Where it is lawful and safe to do so, the account holder is told what happened and how to appeal.

9. Appeals

Either party can ask for a review of a decision within 7 days of the written view. Send the request with any evidence that was not available the first time. A second review is carried out against the same record, and the outcome is final within the platform. It does not affect your right to pursue a legal remedy.

10. Contact

Questions about this policy, or a case that needs attention today, can go to gahonsh@gmail.com, the contact page or WhatsApp on +91 88251 83628. Our Privacy Policy explains what we do with the information you send with a dispute.

Dispute, Cancellation and Refund Policy Overview

This page sets out what happens when a marketplace engagement does not go to plan. It covers the three situations that cause almost every support message: a client who needs to stop a project, a freelancer who has not been paid for delivered work, and a disagreement about whether a deliverable matches the brief. The summary below explains the practical sequence, so you can act without waiting for a reply.

Why this policy exists

A marketplace is a promise about process. Two people who have never worked together agree a scope, a price and a date, and both need to know what happens if one of those changes. Without a written process, the party with more time or more leverage wins, and the other leaves. This policy exists so that a small disagreement does not become a lost client or an unpaid freelancer, and so that both sides know the timeline before they start.

Cancellation in one paragraph

Before a proposal is accepted, either side can walk away and nothing is owed. After acceptance, either side can request cancellation from the contract card in the portal, and the request is recorded with a reason. Work that was delivered and accepted stays payable; work that never started is not. A deposit reserves delivery time and is non-refundable once work begins. Cancelling closes the contract, so the project does not sit in the portal looking active when it is not.

Refunds in one paragraph

Refunds follow the milestone checklist. An undelivered milestone that was paid is refundable; a delivered and accepted milestone is not. A change of mind after delivery, or a requirement that was never in the agreed scope, is not a refund case. Because payments move directly between the client and the freelancer, a refund is a transfer back from the freelancer, arranged with the record on this site as the evidence. Gahonsh does not hold funds and cannot reverse a bank or wallet transfer on its own.

Disputes in one paragraph

A dispute is a request for a written view. Send the project link, the milestone, the agreed scope, what was delivered and the outcome you want. We acknowledge within 2 business days and give a written view within 10 business days, after asking both sides for their account. We can mark the contract as disputed, recommend a fix or a refund, and restrict an account that ignores the outcome. We cannot move money, and our view is a recommendation rather than a court order.

How this fits the rest of the site

The scope that a dispute is judged against comes from the project brief and the proposal the client accepted, both of which stay on the project page. The delivery record comes from the milestone checklist on the contract. The conversation comes from the contract message thread, and files shared during the engagement stay with the contract. That is why the advice on this page is always the same: keep the scope specific, keep the discussion in the thread, and mark milestones as they are delivered. A clear record is the difference between a five-minute answer and a two-week argument.

What we will not do

We will not take a side because one party is louder, we will not publish a private dispute, and we will not promise a payment protection scheme the platform does not run. We also will not let a chargeback replace this process: a reversal filed with a bank while a case is open restricts the account until the record is reviewed. If the process above does not resolve your case, your legal rights remain exactly as they were.
